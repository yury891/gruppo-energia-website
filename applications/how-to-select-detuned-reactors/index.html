How to Select Detuned Reactors for Power Factor Correction Systems

Detuned reactors are used with capacitor banks to prevent resonance and protect power factor correction systems in networks affected by harmonic distortion. Correct selection depends on tuning factor, capacitor data, network voltage, harmonic spectrum and reactor design level.

Application Note

Why Detuned Reactors Are Used

Capacitor banks used for reactive power compensation can interact with network impedance and harmonic currents generated by non-linear loads such as variable frequency drives, UPS systems and rectifiers. Under certain conditions, this interaction can create resonance, a condition where harmonic voltages and currents are amplified rather than absorbed, increasing distortion and stressing electrical equipment.

Detuned reactors shift the resonance frequency of the capacitor-reactor LC circuit to a value below the dominant harmonic frequency present in the network. Their primary role is to prevent resonance and protect capacitors, contactors and other switching components from harmonic overcurrents. Detuned reactors do not eliminate harmonics from the network for harmonic mitigation, dedicated filtering solutions are required.

Harmonic Spectrum

Why Harmonic Spectrum Matters More Than THD Alone

THDi and THDu are useful indicators of distortion severity, but they are not sufficient alone for reactor selection. THDi expresses total harmonic current distortion as a percentage of the fundamental current, while THDu expresses the resulting voltage distortion at the point of measurement. Both values depend heavily on the individual harmonic components present in the network.

Reactor selection must consider the individual harmonic orders — 3rd, 5th, 7th, 11th, 13th and higher — and their relative magnitudes. The 5th harmonic is dominant in most industrial networks with 6-pulse drives, while the 3rd harmonic may be significant in installations with single-phase loads or unbalanced systems.

Important: Two installations can have the same THDi but completely different harmonic spectra, and therefore may require different tuning factors and reactor design levels. Always evaluate the full harmonic spectrum before selecting a reactor.

Tuning Factor

Choosing the Tuning Factor: 5.67%, 7% or 14%

The detuning factor defines the resonance frequency of the LC circuit it does not define reactor robustness or harmonic capability. A higher tuning factor means a lower resonance frequency, which places the tuned circuit further from the dominant harmonics. Selection depends on the harmonic spectrum present in the network, not on a general rule of "clean" versus "dirty" networks.

A 14% tuning factor is not reserved only for highly distorted networks. It is selected when the resonance frequency must be placed below low-order harmonics such as the 3rd harmonic, for example in installations with low-order harmonic components, unbalanced loads or specific harmonic conditions identified by measurement.

Tuning Factor Tuning Order Resonance Freq. at 50 Hz Resonance Freq. at 60 Hz Typical Use
5.67% 4.2 ~210 Hz ~252 Hz Clean or lightly distorted networks
7% 3.8 ~189 Hz ~227 Hz Industrial networks with VFDs and dominant 5th harmonic
14% 2.7 ~134 Hz ~160 Hz Mixed harmonic sources or low-order harmonic presence

Design Level

Tuning Factor Is Not the Same as Reactor Robustness

Tuning factor and design level are two independent selection parameters. The tuning factor (5.67%, 7%, 14%) defines the resonance frequency of the LC circuit. The design level (Standard, Heavy Duty, Extra Heavy Duty) defines the construction robustness of the reactor its saturation current, linearity and thermal capability.

A 7% reactor can be Standard, Heavy Duty or Extra Heavy Duty. Selecting the correct design level depends on the harmonic current stress, ambient temperature and duty cycle of the installation not on the tuning factor alone.

Capacitor Matching

Matching Reactors with Capacitors

The inductance of a detuned reactor must be calculated in relation to the capacitor it is paired with. The resonance frequency of the LC circuit depends on both the capacitance value and the reactor inductance not on the kVAr rating alone. Two approaches are available depending on whether the capacitor bank is new or existing.

New PFC Systems

GE-RTM3 — Reactor + Capacitor Matched Units

Reactor and capacitor are engineered together as a complete LC step. Used when the system is designed from scratch to achieve the required kVAr output and tuning frequency. Optimized LC configuration, accurate kVAr output and reduced mismatch risk.

Retrofit Applications

GE-RT3 — Reactors for Existing Capacitor Banks

Reactor inductance is calculated according to the installed capacitance, network voltage and required tuning factor. Used when the capacitor bank already exists and only the reactor must be added or replaced.

Example: For a 7% detuned step at 400 V requiring 25 kVAr, reactor and capacitor must be calculated together as a matched unit. If an existing 25 kVAr capacitor rated at 440 V is used instead, the actual capacitance will differ from the nominal kVAr value at 400 V and the required reactor inductance must be recalculated accordingly.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes in Detuned Reactor Selection

The following errors are frequently encountered in detuned reactor selection and can result in resonance, component failure or suboptimal system performance.

Selecting the reactor based only on kVAr rating, without considering actual capacitor capacitance

Ignoring the actual capacitor capacitance value when calculating required inductance

Confusing tuning factor with harmonic capability a higher tuning factor does not mean higher robustness

Using a Standard design reactor in installations with high harmonic stress or high ambient temperature

Assuming that detuned reactors reduce THDu or THDi they prevent resonance but do not filter harmonics

Ignoring 3rd harmonic or zero-sequence harmonic components present in unbalanced or single-phase-dominated networks

Harmonic Mitigation

When a Detuned Reactor Is Not Enough

Detuned reactors are designed to prevent resonance and protect capacitor banks they are not designed as harmonic mitigation filters. If the objective is to reduce THDi or THDu to comply with grid standards or equipment requirements, detuned reactors alone will not achieve this.

For harmonic mitigation, passive harmonic filters or active harmonic filters may be required depending on the load profile, harmonic spectrum, system impedance and the target THD levels. The appropriate solution must be determined through a power quality analysis of the installation.
